Television Energy Consumption

Understanding the energy impact of TVs in Australian homes

TV Energy Usage

Televisions are a common appliance in Australian households, with many homes having multiple TVs. Modern televisions, especially LED and OLED models, are significantly more energy-efficient than older CRT or plasma models.

A typical modern LED TV consumes between 50-150 watts depending on screen size and technology. Larger screens and OLED technology generally consume more energy.

Energy Comparison by TV Type

  • LED/LCD (32 inches): ~50-70 watts
  • LED/LCD (55 inches): ~80-120 watts
  • OLED (55 inches): ~100-150 watts
  • Plasma (older): ~200-300 watts

Tips to Reduce TV Energy Consumption

  • Adjust brightness: Lower brightness settings can significantly reduce energy usage.
  • Use power-saving mode: Most modern TVs have energy-saving features.
  • Turn off when not in use: Don't leave TVs on standby for extended periods.
  • Consider screen size: Larger screens consume more energy.
  • Choose energy-efficient models: Look for TVs with high energy star ratings.

Energy Consumption Trends

The trend toward larger screens and higher resolution displays (4K, 8K) has increased energy consumption in recent years. However, advancements in display technology, particularly OLED and QLED, have improved energy efficiency.

According to recent data, the average Australian household spends approximately $100-150 per year on electricity to power their televisions, depending on usage patterns and TV efficiency.
